# Problem statement: Supply Chain Orchestra

**Tagline.** Every Product Has a Journey.

## Question
A product reaches a shopper through two decisions that interact: **where to hold stock** (which distribution sites to open) and **how to move it**
(which plant ships how many tons of which commodity through which site to which city). This product answers, on a transparent synthetic network
anchored to real US cities:

1. **Design.** Which candidate sites should be open, and what flows result, at minimum total annual cost (fixed site cost + line-haul + last-mile + handling)?
2. **Why.** Which constraints are binding (site capacity, plant supply, the open-site limit), and what is the shadow price of relaxing them?
3. **When it breaks.** If the scenario is infeasible (demand above plant supply, capacity too small, too few sites allowed) the product says which constraints had to be
   relaxed and by how much, via a documented elastic relaxation, instead of returning an opaque "infeasible".
4. **Robustness.** How do a legacy footprint and the optimized footprint behave under seeded random outages of sites and lanes and under demand shocks?

The same MILP is solved in Python (reference) and in the browser (HiGHS-wasm). Both build the identical LP text; a hash and objective parity vectors prove it.

## Honesty position
The network is **SYNTHETIC**. Only two things are real: each demand node's **population** (Census PEP 2025, OBSERVED) and its **location**
(Census Gazetteer internal point, OBSERVED; not a city hall, not a warehouse). Sites, plants, commodity volumes, costs, capacities, outages and
shocks are generated by documented rules with a fixed seed. Freight rates are assumptions: no verified public freight-rate series exists
(see DATA_SOURCES.md). Nothing here is a statement about any real company's network or any real freight price.
Distances are great-circle miles times a circuity factor of 1.2; there is no road routing.

## Scope
In: 100 largest contiguous-US places as demand nodes, 14 candidate sites chosen from the 60 largest, 3 plants, 3 commodities, two-echelon flow,
fixed-charge facility location, capacity and supply limits, an open-site cap, scenario controls, elastic infeasibility explanation, seeded Monte Carlo of
site/lane outages and demand shocks.
Out (stated, not hidden): road routing and transit time, inventory and safety stock, multi-period dynamics, lead times, CO2, real freight rates, real facility
locations or costs, Alaska/Hawaii/Puerto Rico, stochastic programming (disruptions are evaluated, not optimized against), live data.

## Success criteria (checked in `tests/`)
- Demand balance per demand node and commodity; flow conservation at every site; capacity and supply respected.
- The published route list reproduces the solved flows; the objective equals the sum of its cost components recomputed from the routes.
- Infeasibility on a constructed shortfall is detected and explained by the elastic relaxation.
- Seeded reproducibility: two runs give byte-identical artifacts.
- Browser parity vectors (generator inputs, LP text hash, status, objective), including infeasible and relaxed cases, reproduce in HiGHS-wasm.
- Every artifact validates against `data/contracts/*.schema.json` and is under 400 KB gzip.

## Honesty tags
OBSERVED (population, internal-point coordinates), DERIVED (distances from observed coordinates), SYNTHETIC (everything generated or solved on the synthetic
network, including all costs and Monte Carlo outcomes). SIMULATED is used for Monte Carlo outcomes in combination (`SYNTHETIC/SIMULATED`). No PREDICTED values.
